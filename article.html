How often should you actually get a fade?

Oct 18, 20266 min readBy Marcus Hale

The short answer is every two to four weeks. The useful answer depends on where the fade starts, how quickly your hair grows and whether you need it to look freshly cut or simply intentional.

Low fades buy you time

A low fade keeps the transition close to the ear and leaves more weight above it. As it grows, the silhouette changes slowly. Most clients can comfortably wait three to four weeks.

High fades show growth sooner

A high skin fade creates more contrast. That contrast is why it looks sharp on day one and why growth becomes visible around day ten. For consistently crisp results, book every two to three weeks.

The practical VANTA rule: schedule the next visit for the day your cut usually starts to bother you, then move it two days earlier.

Choose a cycle, not emergency appointments

  • Low fade: every 3–4 weeks
  • Mid fade: every 2–3 weeks
  • High or skin fade: every 2 weeks
  • Scissor cut: every 4–6 weeks

A recurring cycle also means your barber works from a known shape instead of repairing a completely grown-out cut every time.

Why the chairs are 1.8 m apart

Space gives the barber a full working angle, keeps neighbouring conversations private and makes the studio feel calm instead of crowded.

Clay, pomade, paste

Use clay for texture and low shine, pomade for control and polish, and paste when you want flexible hold that can be restyled during the day.

Why we still use a straight razor

A trimmer creates a close edge. A properly prepared straight razor creates a clean one and removes the final shadow around a line-up.

The 8-week beard plan

Do not shape the beard aggressively in the first four weeks. Keep the neckline clean, use a light oil and let density reveal itself before choosing the final outline.

Cutting curls

Curls should be assessed in their natural, dry position. Shrinkage and direction matter more than a perfectly even wet perimeter.

A home trimmer

For upkeep, prioritise an adjustable blade, replaceable guards and a body you can control around the neckline. Expensive does not automatically mean precise.

The studio playlist

The soundtrack stays rhythmic but quiet enough for consultation. No podcasts: a shared conversation should never compete with another voice.

Three cuts for thinning hair

Short texture, a controlled crop and a clean buzz all reduce contrast between dense and sparse areas. Added length rarely hides the problem.

Inside the VANTA exam

The internal assessment covers consultation, sanitation, sectioning, clipper transitions, scissor control, razor safety and the final client handoff.
